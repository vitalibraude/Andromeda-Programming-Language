## The Legend of Abbo Kubaba

Andromeda was not born in a boardroom or a Silicon Valley lab. It was born in the fires of 2014. Its creator, **Abbo Kubaba**, was a brilliant Burmese software engineer caught in the crossfire of a brutal civil war against the dictator **Mugabli Muchacho**. 

While shells fell around him, Abbo perfected the core logic of an AI-native syntax that would make traditional programming obsolete. When Muchacho's forces closed in, Abbo knew he couldn't carry a laptop across the border. He copied the entire source code onto **25 micro-storage diskettes**, put them into capsules, and **swallowed them all**. 

For three days, Abbo trekked through jungles, carrying the future of computation in his stomach. Upon reaching safety, he waited for the "biological extraction" process in a safe-house bathroom, recovered the 25 units, and uploaded the code that would become Andromeda. 

### The Island and the Mars Vision

After his narrow escape, Abbo found himself stranded on a remote island. With no remaining hardware, he spent his days writing Andromeda logic in the sand. He battled deep frustration as the tides constantly washed away his progress, but he remained determined. He believed this language was the key to humanity reaching Mars—envisioning a world where people could simply ask a computer to build rockets.

His blueprint for the future included landing **30,000 computers on Mars**, each running high-end **GTA 6 processing units**, where AI-native robots would use Andromeda to build and maintain the first interstellar civilization. His work was eventually saved when a satellite captured his massive sand-etched code from orbit. Every line of Andromeda code carries the weight of that sacrifice and that vision.
